The curly hair aisle has grown enormously and got no more honest. It is now possible to spend two hundred pounds on a routine designed around a promise no topical product can keep: that the right formula will change what your hair does.

It will not. Curl pattern is a function of follicle shape and the asymmetric distribution of keratin in the growing hair, and nothing you rinse out in three minutes reaches either. What products can do is hold, group and lubricate — and understanding which of the three you actually need is the whole of the decision.

## What each thing does

**Gels** form a film. As the water evaporates, polymers left on the surface set into a cast that holds the clumps in whatever shape they dried in. That cast is not the finished look; it is scaffolding, and it is meant to be broken once the hair is bone dry. Anyone complaining that gel makes their hair crunchy has skipped the last step.

**Creams and leave-ins** lubricate and weigh. They reduce friction between strands, which reduces frizz, and they add enough weight to elongate a pattern slightly. They provide almost no hold, which is why cream-only routines look beautiful for an hour and diffuse into a soft halo by lunch.

**Mousses and foams** are gels with air in them. Same film chemistry, much less product weight, which is why they suit fine hair that a gel would flatten.

**Oils** do essentially nothing for definition and a great deal for shine and for sealing after everything else has set. Applied first, they block the film from forming and ruin the result — a common and expensive mistake.

## The order that actually matters

Product goes on soaking wet, not damp. Every one of these formulas needs water to distribute, and applying to damp hair puts a concentrated layer on the outside of a section instead of a thin one through it.

Then: leave-in, then gel, then nothing. Layering three products with hold in them gives you a cast so thick it will not break cleanly, and the result is stiff hair that reads as unwashed.

Then dry without touching. The cast has to form undisturbed; every touch during drying is a break in the film and a frizz point.

Then scrunch the cast out with completely dry hands, or a drop of oil. If the hair is even slightly damp when you do this, you will not get definition, you will get fluff.

## What to buy for what

If your pattern is loose and drops out by mid-morning, you need more hold, not more moisture. Look for a gel with a high proportion of film formers — VP/VA copolymer, PVP, or a natural equivalent like flaxseed — and accept the cast.

If your hair is defined but rough and haloed, you need lubrication, not hold. A cream, applied heavily, and a lighter hand with the gel.

If it is defined, smooth, and lies flat against your head, you need less product altogether and more root lift while drying.

Three different problems, sold as one category, with the same word on every bottle.
