Somewhere in the last fifteen years the signature scent stopped being the goal. The recommended arrangement now is a wardrobe: something fresh for the office, something warm for evening, a summer one, a winter one, a "skin scent" for days you want to smell of nothing in particular.

It is a lovely idea and it is, transparently, a commercial one. Five bottles instead of one.

I have owned the wardrobe. I have gone back to one bottle, and the argument for it is not frugality.

## Scent is the only sense wired for memory that way

Olfactory signals reach the amygdala and hippocampus with fewer synapses in between than any other sensory modality. That anatomical shortcut is why smell retrieves memory more vividly and more involuntarily than sight or sound — and why the association forms at all.

But an association needs repetition to form. A fragrance worn eleven times in a year is a nice smell. A fragrance worn four hundred times becomes attached to a period of your life, and it will still be doing that in twenty years.

A wardrobe of five gets you five nice smells and no attachments. That is the trade, and it is rarely stated.

## What you lose in a wardrobe

The ability to notice. Wearing one fragrance consistently, you become extremely sensitive to how it varies — with heat, with your own skin, with how much sleep you have had. It stops being a static object and becomes something you have a relationship with. Rotating five, you never get past the surface of any of them.

Other people's recognition, which matters more than it sounds. Being identifiable by scent is one of the more intimate things available in ordinary life, and it requires consistency to exist at all.

And, practically: five bottles means four of them are oxidising in a cupboard. Fragrance degrades in light, heat and air, and a bottle worn twice a season is losing its top notes to headspace and time. The wardrobe's economics are worse than they look.

## The honest objections

Some people genuinely cannot wear one composition every day — anosmia to your own signature is real, though it is mostly perceptual habituation rather than true fatigue, and other people still smell it perfectly well.

And some fragrances really are seasonally unwearable. A heavy oriental at thirty degrees is unpleasant for you and everyone within a metre. If your signature is one of those, a second for summer is not a wardrobe, it is a coat.

## How to find one worth committing to

Wear a sample for a fortnight before buying anything. Not a spritz and a decision — fourteen days, at your usual dose, in your usual life. Almost everything is exciting for three days.

Then ask the only question that matters: on the eleventh day, when you have stopped noticing it, do you want to put it on again?

If yes, buy the largest bottle. If no, you have saved yourself a shelf.
