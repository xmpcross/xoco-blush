The most common question we get is a version of the same one: something changed, I do not know what caused it, what should I do. And almost every time, the reason it cannot be answered is that four things changed at once.

Skincare is one of the few consumer categories where the product only works if you can attribute the result to it, and where nearly everyone makes attribution impossible on purpose.

## Why the confound is so easy to create

New products arrive in clusters. A recommendation, a sale, an empty bottle and a restock, and suddenly the routine has three items in it that were not there a fortnight ago.

Skin also has a long lag. Cell turnover in adult skin runs roughly forty days, and most actives have a meaningful lead time — twelve weeks for a retinoid, six to eight for a vitamin C, four for a good moisturiser. A change introduced in week one shows up in week eight, by which time two more things have been added and the timeline is unrecoverable.

And skin varies on its own. Season, hormonal cycle, sleep, illness, water hardness after a house move. Any of those will produce a swing large enough to be mistaken for a product effect.

## The method

Change one thing. Wait the appropriate length of time for that thing. Then change the next.

For a moisturiser or a cleanser: four weeks. For an active: twelve. Those are not conservative numbers, they are the intervals at which the published trials for these ingredients measured anything.

Photograph, in the same place, at the same time of day, without makeup. Human memory for a face is dreadful, and the improvement you are trying to detect is the size of the difference between two mornings. Photographs are the only reason my own experiments have ever produced a number I trusted.

Write down the date you started. Every single time. It costs four seconds and it is the difference between an experiment and an anecdote.

## What to do with a shelf that is already crowded

Strip it back. Cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen, for two weeks. This is not a detox — skin does not detox and nothing is being purged — it is a baseline, and it is the only way to find out what your skin is like without eight variables on it.

A great many people discover at this point that two of the eight were causing the problem they were buying the other six to fix.

Then reintroduce, one at a time, at the intervals above. It will take four months to rebuild a routine this way. You will end it knowing what each item does, which is more than almost anyone can say about the shelf they have now.

## The uncomfortable part

This method makes it much harder to buy things, which is presumably why nobody selling anything recommends it.

A routine you can test is a routine that grows slowly, rejects most of what it tries, and ends up short. That is not an accident of the method. It is the finding.
