Air drying is treated as the absence of styling: you skip the dryer, you let it do what it does, and whatever happens is your hair's natural state. It is not. Air drying is a styling method with a very short window, and the reason most people conclude their hair "does not air dry well" is that they spent that window doing other things.

Hydrogen bonds in keratin break when hair is wet and reform as it dries. Whatever shape the strand is holding while that happens is the shape it keeps until it is wetted again. That process is largely finished by the time hair is around 70 per cent dry, which on medium-density hair is somewhere between fifteen and thirty minutes. Everything that happens after that is maintenance. Everything that happens before it is the haircut you are going to walk around in.

## What to do in the first twenty minutes

Get the water out without disturbing the pattern. Squeeze, do not rub — a towel dragged along the shaft lifts the cuticle and that is most of what people call frizz. A cotton T-shirt or a microfibre towel, pressed rather than tousled, removes enough water to shorten drying time without touching the shape.

Then apply product to hair that is genuinely wet rather than damp. Every leave-in, cream and gel on the market distributes properly through water and unevenly through anything drier. If you have waited until it is towel-dry, you are laying product on top of sections instead of through them.

Then, and this is the part that gets skipped: stop touching it. Any manipulation while those bonds are reforming breaks the clumps that give definition and introduces the frizz you are about to blame on humidity.

## The single most useful adjustment

Where the hair sits while it dries matters more than any product in the routine.

Hair drying flat against the head dries flat. If you want volume at the root, the root has to be lifted while it sets — clips at the crown, a few minutes upside down, or simply parting it on the opposite side for the first ten minutes and moving it back afterwards. If you want a smoother result, the opposite: keep tension on the lengths, twist sections loosely, and do not let it dry in the shape a pillow gives it.

None of this requires a product. All of it requires being slightly deliberate for twenty minutes.

## Where air drying genuinely does not work

On fine, straight hair that dries in eight minutes, there is not enough time for any of this to matter much, and a dryer on cool with a nozzle will give you more shape in less time.

On very coarse or high-density hair, air drying can take four hours, and hair kept wet that long is more prone to hygral fatigue — repeated swelling and contraction that weakens the cortex over months. If your hair is still damp after two hours, finishing it with a diffuser on low heat is not cheating. It is the more protective option.

The rest of us have twenty minutes to make a decision, and we have been spending them on our phones.
