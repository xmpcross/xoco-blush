Home gel has been the most improved category in beauty over the last five years and it is still the one where the gap between the good part and the bad part is widest. Nocturne is a clean demonstration of both halves.

## The application

Excellent, and better than most salon-grade polishes we have used at home. The brush is wide and flat, which sounds trivial and is the single biggest determinant of whether an amateur can lay a smooth coat: three strokes covers a nail, and three strokes means fewer overlaps and no ridge lines.

Opacity in two coats is the real achievement. Deep shades in gel are usually two-and-a-half coats in practice — a second coat that is nearly there, and a patchy third to fix it. This one is genuinely opaque on the second, with no streaking and no thin patch at the cuticle.

The one application caveat is curing distance. At the recommended sixty seconds, with the hand pressed flat into the lamp, it cured hard. Held a couple of centimetres higher — which is what people do without noticing — the surface stayed tacky and needed a second cure.

## The wear

Nine days without a chip, across three applications, on hands that wash constantly and load a dishwasher daily. That is at the top of what home gel achieves. Tip wear was visible under direct light from day seven, in the sense that the free edge had gone very slightly matte; it was not visible in a room.

No lifting at the cuticle, which is where most home applications fail by day four. That is a formulation and a prep story in equal measure, and the prep instructions in the box are good.

## The removal

And here it falls apart.

Acetone, foil wraps, twenty minutes, then a second twenty because the first had barely softened it. Then scraping, which is where the damage happens: four nails out of ten came away with a visibly rough, white, thinned surface that took three weeks of growth to leave.

Some of that is the nature of gel and some of it is avoidable. A flexible base coat designed for easier release would take most of it away, and Nocturne sells one — it is simply not mentioned anywhere in the instructions that come with the polish, which walk you straight into the harder removal.

That omission is the difference between a four-star product and a three-star one. The formula is not the problem. The document is.

## The shade

Ink Plum is very good: a deep cool purple with enough blue in it to read almost black indoors and unmistakably purple in daylight. It is the sort of shade that usually needs three coats and does not here, which is most of why we would buy it again for an occasion.

Just not for a fortnightly habit, and not on nails that are already having a hard time.
